Overview

Sometimes the point of camp dinner is the grill, not the fastest possible boil.

Used on my Oahu trip and it punched above its weight. The steel walls hold heat, so you do not have to dump a chimney to get dinner done. It folds down into a tidy slab that rides in the truck without rattling, then pops together fast at camp. It is not ultralight - it is real steel with a powder coat - but the tradeoff is sturdy, even heat and a grate that feels trustworthy.

Best for Car camping, beach cookouts, simple steak and vegetable dinners, tailgates, and trips where charcoal cleanup is acceptable.
Not for Backpacking, fire restrictions, rushed meals, or anyone who hates dealing with ash.

If you want a compact grill that actually grills - with real sear and low charcoal use - this is an easy win.

My Notes

This belongs in the car-camp lane. It is not efficient trail gear; it is for nights where dinner being good is part of the trip.

  • Bring a legal ash plan before lighting it.
  • Use less charcoal than your backyard brain wants.
  • Do not make this your only cooking option when fire rules are uncertain.
