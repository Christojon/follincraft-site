Start with the meter’s own instructions

The step-by-step lesson is being rebuilt. The previous instructions and unverified demonstration images have been withdrawn. These limited reference notes are not a substitute for an equipment-specific demonstration.

Use the exact meter manual to identify COM, the voltage input, the selected function and the permitted range. A current input is not interchangeable with the voltage input. Never connect a meter configured for current directly across a battery or supply.

Continuity and resistance checks require an appropriately de-energized, isolated test item. Turning a switch off is not proof that a circuit is safe. These notes do not teach household mains, vehicle high-voltage systems, current measurement or work on unknown circuits. Fluke’s measurement reference explains why each function needs a different setup.

Voltage is not battery health

A resting voltage reading does not establish cranking capacity, remaining life or whether a charging system is working correctly. Chemistry, temperature, recent charging and vehicle charging strategy affect interpretation. The old table’s universal pass/fail implications have been removed.

A car battery is not the beginner exercise here: low voltage can still deliver dangerous fault current. Repeated no-starts need a vehicle-appropriate battery and charging-system assessment. Do not diagnose a bad cable from two unloaded voltage readings or keep cranking to make a number change.
