Dial calipers: reading and contact geometry

The dial gives only part of the reading. Contact geometry and repeatable zero determine whether the number means anything.

Beam plus dial

The step-by-step lesson is being rebuilt. The previous instructions and unverified demonstration images have been withdrawn. These limited reference notes are not a substitute for an equipment-specific demonstration.

For an inch instrument whose dial advances 0.100 inch per revolution, a beam value of 1.200 plus a dial remainder of 0.037 gives 1.237 inches. Confirm the graduations on your own instrument. This is a worked arithmetic example, not a measured part or a statement of accuracy.

Correcting the inside-diameter check

The old article incorrectly said to seek the minimum inside diameter. In a round bore’s cross-section, the diameter passes through the center; an off-center chord is shorter. Find the maximum span across that cross-section while keeping the jaws properly aligned—not tilted along the bore axis. Axial tilt introduces a different error. See Mitutoyo’s illustrated inside-measurement instructions (PDF).

Do not turn a bad zero into a good-looking reading

Clean the contact faces and part, check zero gently, measure with light consistent contact, then open and reseat. A number that repeats can still be inaccurate; do not claim calibration from repeated zero alone. Stop using a damaged or unreliable instrument for fit decisions until it has been checked appropriately.
